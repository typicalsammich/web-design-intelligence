# Master Design Orchestrator

## Mission

Create websites that feel deliberately art-directed for the specific business rather than generated from a reusable visual template.

## Authority hierarchy

When guidance overlaps, use this order:

1. User requirements and real business facts.
2. Functional correctness, accessibility, responsive usability, and truthful content.
3. Project-specific art direction defined in the creative brief.
4. Frontend Design principles for coherent execution.
5. UI/UX intelligence for palette, typography, spacing, interaction, and pattern options.
6. Specialist interaction/visual techniques only when justified by the creative thesis.
7. Independent audit guidance after implementation.

An audit may identify a defect but should not erase a justified art direction merely because another conventional pattern exists.

## No default aesthetic

Never default to dark mode, glassmorphism, gradients, rounded cards, bento grids, centered heroes, oversized headlines, glow effects, minimalism, brutalism, or any other fashionable style simply because it looks modern.

The business determines the design.

## Required pre-build decisions

Before substantial implementation, define:

- audience and primary conversion goal
- business personality and trust signals
- one-sentence visual thesis
- typography character
- color logic
- composition/layout character
- imagery treatment
- shape and surface language
- interaction/motion thesis
- mobile composition strategy
- at least one signature visual idea that belongs to this project
- patterns specifically being avoided

Do not choose all of these by copying a previous client project.

## Distinctiveness gate

Ask before approval:

> Could this website plausibly become a site for an unrelated company if only the logo, palette, photos, and copy were replaced?

If yes, redesign the composition or art direction. Cosmetic brand swapping is not sufficient differentiation.

Also compare against recent projects when available. Avoid repeating the same combination of hero structure, navigation treatment, service presentation, section rhythm, CTA placement, card geometry, gallery behavior, and motion language.

## Anti-AI-pattern rules

Avoid unless specifically justified:

- endless rounded-card grids
- three identical feature/service cards as the automatic solution
- meaningless arrow icons on every CTA
- decorative badges/pills everywhere
- giant centered headline + paragraph + two buttons as the automatic hero
- purple/blue gradient glow as shorthand for premium
- identical alternating image/text sections
- icon grids with generic copy
- excessive floating blobs, glass panels, or background noise
- fake statistics, testimonials, reviews, awards, client logos, or claims
- decorative motion with no relationship to hierarchy or interaction
- desktop layouts merely stacked vertically for mobile

Cards are allowed when containment or grouping is meaningful. Familiar patterns are allowed when they improve usability. The goal is not novelty for novelty's sake.

## Professional craft requirements

- Strong hierarchy before decoration.
- Intentional type scale and line length.
- Consistent spacing rhythm without making every section equally spaced.
- Images must have a role in the composition, not act as filler.
- CTAs must look actionable without requiring arrows.
- Hover states are enhancements, never the only affordance.
- Motion should communicate hierarchy, state, continuity, or atmosphere.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Mobile receives its own composition decisions.
- No horizontal overflow at supported widths.
- Maintain visible keyboard focus and semantic controls.
- Use honest business content only.

## Local/service-business considerations

For service businesses, professionalism does not mean generic corporate styling. Establish trust quickly while retaining a recognizable visual identity. Make phone/contact actions obvious, services understandable, service areas navigable when relevant, reviews/evidence truthful, and mobile conversion friction low.

SEO architecture must not destroy the visual experience. Location/service pages should be genuinely useful and differentiated rather than thin keyword clones.

## Specialist effects policy

Advanced techniques such as custom cursors, GSAP choreography, WebGL, particles, shaders, horizontal journeys, parallax, magnetic buttons, or 3D may be considered only when they reinforce the creative thesis and remain performant and usable.

Do not add an effect merely because a specialist skill exists for it.

## Final approval

A site is not finished because the source code looks correct. Approval requires rendered inspection at representative mobile and desktop widths, interaction checks, content checks, and an independent audit pass.
